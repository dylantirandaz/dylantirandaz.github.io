Periapical Lesions Bench

By Dylan Tirandaz, Prime Intellect RL ResidentNovember 13, 2026

Periapical Lesions are pathological changes or breakdowns of tissue around the root tip of the tooth. This type of infection typically occurs at the tip (apex) of a tooth's root where the tooth's nerve and blood vessels exit into the surrounding jawbone. Dentists find them on X-rays and grade them according to the Periapical Index (PAI). These X-rays are of the whole jaw and are usually 2,400 pixels wide, but a typical lesion is usually only 60 pixels small. And agents struggle with it. The best model, Gemini Carbon, only achieves a 16% pass@1 and most models struggle to get past a 10% pass rate!

Task Shape

The model is put into a similar setting as a dentist. It gets a panoramic film of the jaw, a short referral note, a codebook explaining the grades, and a chart of example lesions for grade 3, 4, and 5 on the PAI. To submit its answer it must file a report and localize every lesion along with the corresponding PAI grade. It is sandboxed with no internet, all it can do is crop/zoom/enhance the film with python as much as it pleases. Scoring only factors two things:

  1. Did it box the correct place, a box counts if it overlaps the dentist’s box by atleast 30%. Extra boxes are penalized
  2. Did it also flag the correct PAI grade for the lesion.

Scoring is binary so in order to pass it must have found every lesion, correctly graded, with no extra boxes. There is at least one lesion in each film so there is a sentence in the prompt to let it know.

The Data

I used Open Source data from Hanoi Medical University (Do et al., 2024, CC BY 4.0), 3,863 panoramic films all with dentist authored gold (localized lesions and corresponding grades). This Data was split into 3,087/389/387 train/validation/test splits with near duplicate films removed and look-alikes kept in the same split.

Setup

I benchmarked six models: Gemini 4 Carbon, GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5, Kimi K3.1 Pro, GLM 5.3 Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. Every model runs in the same coding harness, I chose Pi, and at high reasoning effort with a 100 turn limit. I used a random seed of 100 films from my test split and ran 3 attempts for each model. Attempts that failed from provider/network/infrastructure errors were rerun.

Failure Modes

The most common failure across all models is the wrong tooth; on the low end 114/300 of Gemini Carbon’s localizations matched no real lesions and more embarrassingly 280/300(!!) of Deepseeks reports matched no real lesions.

"But perhaps actual lesion at 36 would be missed. Could include both, scoring precision perhaps harmed. Better one most likely."

Another funny failure mode is with GLM 5.3 would draw very big boxes, compared to the dentists small and precise localizations. GLMs boxes had a median area 2.7 times larger than the dentists. On one attempt it found the right molar and drew a 210x200 px box around a 83x77 px lesion, getting itself a zero. Carbon Sol and Opus on the other hand was more precise, a box covered a lesion's centre but missed on size in 39 GLM attempts, 38 Kimi, 18 Opus, 14 Sol and 12 Carbon.
